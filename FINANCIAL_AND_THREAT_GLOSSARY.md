# Financial & Threat Intelligence Glossary 💰🔒

## Financial Terms

### Funding Stages

#### **Seed Round**
- **What it is:** Initial funding to start the company
- **Typical amount:** $500K - $2M
- **Purpose:** Build MVP (Minimum Viable Product), validate idea
- **Investors:** Angel investors, early-stage VCs
- **Company stage:** Pre-product or early product
- **Example:** "SafeHill raised $1.6M in Seed funding"

#### **Series A**
- **What it is:** First major institutional funding round
- **Typical amount:** $2M - $15M
- **Purpose:** Scale product, grow team, acquire customers
- **Investors:** Venture capital firms
- **Company stage:** Product-market fit achieved
- **Example:** "Descope raised $52.8M in Series A"

#### **Series A-Prime** (or A+)
- **What it is:** Additional funding between Series A and B
- **Typical amount:** $5M - $20M
- **Purpose:** Extend runway, hit milestones before Series B
- **Why it happens:** Need more capital before ready for Series B
- **Example:** "Mondoo raised $10.5M in Series A-Prime"

#### **Series B**
- **What it is:** Growth funding to scale operations
- **Typical amount:** $15M - $50M
- **Purpose:** Expand market, scale sales, enter new markets
- **Investors:** Growth-stage VCs
- **Company stage:** Proven business model, growing revenue
- **Example:** "SEON raised $48M in Series B"

#### **Series C**
- **What it is:** Late-stage funding for major expansion
- **Typical amount:** $30M - $100M+
- **Purpose:** Scale globally, acquisitions, prepare for IPO
- **Investors:** Late-stage VCs, private equity
- **Company stage:** Established company, strong revenue
- **Example:** "Company X raised $75M in Series C"

#### **Series D+**
- **What it is:** Additional late-stage rounds
- **Typical amount:** $50M - $200M+
- **Purpose:** Delay IPO, major expansion, acquisitions
- **Company stage:** Pre-IPO or alternative to IPO
- **Example:** "ID.me raised $204M in Series D"

### Funding Metrics

#### **Total Funding**
- **Definition:** All money raised across all rounds
- **Formula:** Seed + Series A + Series B + Series C + ...
- **Example:** "$88M total funding" = all rounds combined
- **Why it matters:** Shows total capital invested, company scale

#### **Last Round Amount**
- **Definition:** Money raised in most recent funding round
- **Example:** "Last Round: Series A - $10.5M"
- **Why it matters:** Shows current valuation, investor confidence

#### **Funding Velocity**
- **Definition:** How fast a company is raising money
- **Calculation:** (Recent funding / Time period) × 100
- **Example:** "+45% funding velocity" = raising money 45% faster
- **Why it matters:** Indicates growth momentum, market demand

#### **Burn Rate**
- **Definition:** How fast a company spends money
- **Calculation:** Monthly expenses - Monthly revenue
- **Example:** "$500K/month burn rate"
- **Why it matters:** Determines runway (how long money lasts)

#### **Runway**
- **Definition:** How long company can operate with current cash
- **Calculation:** Cash on hand / Monthly burn rate
- **Example:** "18 months runway" = can operate for 18 months
- **Why it matters:** Shows financial health, urgency for next round

#### **Valuation**
- **Definition:** Company's estimated worth
- **Types:** 
  - Pre-money: Value before funding
  - Post-money: Value after funding
- **Example:** "$100M post-money valuation"
- **Why it matters:** Determines investor ownership, exit potential

### Investment Terms

#### **Lead Investor**
- **Definition:** Primary investor in a funding round
- **Role:** Sets terms, largest investment, board seat
- **Example:** "Lightspeed Venture Partners led the round"
- **Why it matters:** Signals quality, provides guidance

#### **Follow-on Investment**
- **Definition:** Existing investor invests in next round
- **Why it happens:** Confidence in company, protect ownership
- **Example:** "Ballistic Ventures participated in Series B"
- **Why it matters:** Shows continued investor support

#### **Down Round**
- **Definition:** Funding at lower valuation than previous round
- **Why it happens:** Company struggling, market downturn
- **Impact:** Dilutes existing shareholders, negative signal
- **Example:** "Series B at $50M (down from $80M Series A)"

#### **Up Round**
- **Definition:** Funding at higher valuation than previous round
- **Why it happens:** Company growing, hitting milestones
- **Impact:** Positive signal, less dilution
- **Example:** "Series B at $120M (up from $40M Series A)"

## Threat Intelligence Terms

### Threat Categories

#### **Advanced Persistent Threat (APT)**
- **Definition:** Sophisticated, long-term cyber attack
- **Characteristics:** State-sponsored, highly skilled, stealthy
- **Target:** Government, critical infrastructure, enterprises
- **Example:** APT29 (Russian state-sponsored group)
- **Why it matters:** Highest threat level, requires advanced defense

#### **Zero-Day Exploit**
- **Definition:** Attack using unknown vulnerability
- **Why "zero-day":** Vendor has 0 days to fix (unknown)
- **Value:** Extremely valuable, sold on black market
- **Example:** "Zero-day in Microsoft Exchange"
- **Why it matters:** No patch available, very dangerous

#### **Ransomware**
- **Definition:** Malware that encrypts data, demands payment
- **How it works:** Encrypt files → Demand Bitcoin → Maybe decrypt
- **Target:** Any organization with valuable data
- **Example:** "WannaCry ransomware attack"
- **Why it matters:** Major threat, costly, disruptive

#### **Phishing**
- **Definition:** Fraudulent emails/messages to steal credentials
- **Types:**
  - Spear phishing: Targeted at specific person
  - Whaling: Targeted at executives
  - Smishing: Via SMS
- **Example:** "Fake Microsoft login page"
- **Why it matters:** #1 attack vector, easy to execute

#### **DDoS (Distributed Denial of Service)**
- **Definition:** Overwhelming system with traffic to crash it
- **How it works:** Botnet sends massive traffic → System crashes
- **Target:** Websites, servers, networks
- **Example:** "GitHub DDoS attack (1.35 Tbps)"
- **Why it matters:** Disrupts business, extortion tool

#### **Insider Threat**
- **Definition:** Threat from within organization
- **Types:**
  - Malicious: Intentional harm
  - Negligent: Accidental exposure
- **Example:** "Employee steals customer data"
- **Why it matters:** Hard to detect, trusted access

### Threat Metrics

#### **Threat Intelligence Score**
- **Definition:** Composite score of threat level (0-100)
- **Factors:** Attack frequency, sophistication, impact
- **Example:** "Threat score: 85 (Very High)"
- **Why it matters:** Prioritize security investments

#### **Attack Surface**
- **Definition:** All possible entry points for attackers
- **Includes:** Networks, applications, devices, people
- **Example:** "Reduced attack surface by 40%"
- **Why it matters:** More surface = more risk

#### **Mean Time to Detect (MTTD)**
- **Definition:** Average time to discover a breach
- **Industry average:** 207 days (2023)
- **Example:** "MTTD: 24 hours"
- **Why it matters:** Faster detection = less damage

#### **Mean Time to Respond (MTTR)**
- **Definition:** Average time to contain/fix breach
- **Industry average:** 73 days (2023)
- **Example:** "MTTR: 4 hours"
- **Why it matters:** Faster response = less damage

#### **False Positive Rate**
- **Definition:** % of alerts that aren't real threats
- **Problem:** Alert fatigue, wasted resources
- **Example:** "False positive rate: 15%"
- **Why it matters:** High rate = missed real threats

### Security Concepts

#### **Zero Trust Architecture**
- **Definition:** "Never trust, always verify" security model
- **Principle:** Verify every user, device, every time
- **Replaces:** Perimeter-based security (castle & moat)
- **Example:** "Implementing zero trust with Okta"
- **Why it matters:** Modern security standard

#### **Endpoint Detection & Response (EDR)**
- **Definition:** Monitor and respond to threats on endpoints
- **Endpoints:** Laptops, desktops, servers, mobile devices
- **Capabilities:** Detect, investigate, respond, remediate
- **Example:** "CrowdStrike Falcon EDR"
- **Why it matters:** Protects distributed workforce

#### **Security Operations Center (SOC)**
- **Definition:** Team that monitors and responds to threats
- **Functions:** 24/7 monitoring, incident response, threat hunting
- **Levels:**
  - Tier 1: Alert triage
  - Tier 2: Investigation
  - Tier 3: Advanced analysis
- **Example:** "Building a SOC team"
- **Why it matters:** Central security command center

#### **Threat Hunting**
- **Definition:** Proactively searching for hidden threats
- **Approach:** Assume breach, look for indicators
- **Tools:** SIEM, EDR, threat intelligence
- **Example:** "Threat hunting found APT in network"
- **Why it matters:** Find threats before they strike

#### **Security Information & Event Management (SIEM)**
- **Definition:** Centralized logging and analysis platform
- **Functions:** Collect logs, correlate events, alert on threats
- **Examples:** Splunk, IBM QRadar, Microsoft Sentinel
- **Why it matters:** Visibility across entire environment

#### **Vulnerability Management**
- **Definition:** Process of finding and fixing security weaknesses
- **Steps:**
  1. Scan for vulnerabilities
  2. Prioritize by risk
  3. Patch/remediate
  4. Verify fix
- **Example:** "Monthly vulnerability scans"
- **Why it matters:** Prevent exploitation

### Compliance & Frameworks

#### **GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation)**
- **What:** EU privacy law
- **Requirements:** Data protection, user consent, breach notification
- **Penalties:** Up to €20M or 4% of revenue
- **Why it matters:** Global privacy standard

#### **CCPA (California Consumer Privacy Act)**
- **What:** California privacy law
- **Requirements:** Data transparency, user rights, opt-out
- **Penalties:** Up to $7,500 per violation
- **Why it matters:** US privacy standard

#### **SOC 2 (Service Organization Control 2)**
- **What:** Security audit framework
- **Criteria:** Security, availability, confidentiality
- **Purpose:** Prove security controls to customers
- **Why it matters:** Required for enterprise sales

#### **ISO 27001**
- **What:** International security standard
- **Requirements:** Information security management system
- **Certification:** Third-party audit
- **Why it matters:** Global security certification

#### **NIST Cybersecurity Framework**
- **What:** US government security framework
- **Functions:** Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover
- **Purpose:** Standardize security practices
- **Why it matters:** Industry best practice

## Combined Financial + Threat Terms

### **Cyber Insurance**
- **Definition:** Insurance against cyber attacks
- **Coverage:** Breach response, legal fees, ransomware
- **Cost:** Based on security posture, revenue
- **Example:** "$5M cyber insurance policy"
- **Why it matters:** Risk transfer, required by customers

### **Security ROI (Return on Investment)**
- **Definition:** Value gained from security investments
- **Calculation:** (Prevented losses - Security costs) / Security costs
- **Example:** "300% security ROI"
- **Why it matters:** Justify security spending

### **Cost of Breach**
- **Definition:** Total cost of a security incident
- **Includes:** Response, legal, fines, lost business, reputation
- **Average:** $4.45M per breach (2023)
- **Example:** "Breach cost company $10M"
- **Why it matters:** Justifies prevention spending

### **Security Budget**
- **Definition:** Annual spending on cybersecurity
- **Typical:** 10-15% of IT budget
- **Allocation:** Tools (40%), People (40%), Services (20%)
- **Example:** "$2M security budget"
- **Why it matters:** Determines security capabilities

## Quick Reference

### Funding Stages (Smallest to Largest)
1. Seed ($500K-$2M)
2. Series A ($2M-$15M)
3. Series B ($15M-$50M)
4. Series C ($30M-$100M+)
5. Series D+ ($50M-$200M+)

### Threat Severity (Lowest to Highest)
1. Low: Minimal impact, easy to fix
2. Medium: Moderate impact, requires attention
3. High: Significant impact, urgent fix needed
4. Critical: Severe impact, immediate action required
5. APT: Nation-state level, ongoing campaign

### Security Maturity Levels
1. **Initial:** Ad-hoc, reactive
2. **Developing:** Some processes, inconsistent
3. **Defined:** Documented processes, consistent
4. **Managed:** Measured, monitored
5. **Optimized:** Continuous improvement, proactive
